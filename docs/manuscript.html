Abstract

Background: Chronic heart failure (CHF) lacks comprehensive clinical interventions. Diverse Asian medicinal plants exert multi-target therapeutic effects on CHF, while the molecular mechanism of the medicinal plant pair Astragali Radix–Aconiti Lateralis Radix Praeparata (AR-ALRP) remains unclear. 1. Introduction

Chronic heart failure (CHF) is a progressive and refractory cardiovascular syndrome characterized by irreversible myocardial remodeling, persistent inflammatory activation, energy metabolism dysfunction and immune microenvironment imbalance, and it remains a leading cause of cardiovascular hospitalization and mortality worldwide . The 2022 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) CHF guidelines have standardized the novel quadruple therapy consisting of angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI)/angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEI)/angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB), β-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRA), and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, which has substantially improved the long-term prognosis of CHF patients . However, constrained by the single-target intervention characteristics of Western medicine, a large proportion of CHF patients still suffer from persistent symptoms, exercise intolerance and continuous disease progression, accompanied by uncorrected myocardial energy metabolism disorders and low-grade chronic inflammation . Given the limitations of monotherapy, exploring complementary multi-pathway and multi-target therapeutic strategies has become a research hotspot for optimizing comprehensive CHF management.

Traditional Chinese medicinal plant pairs exhibit unique holistic advantages in treating complex cardiovascular diseases due to their multi-component, multi-target and synergistic regulatory properties, which effectively compensate for the single-mechanism defects of synthetic Western drugs . Growing pharmacological and clinical evidence confirms that bioactive metabolites from medicinal plants can comprehensively intervene in core pathological links of CHF, including myocardial inflammation, apoptosis, fibrosis and energy metabolism dysfunction, and produce synergistic cardioprotective effects with first-line clinical drugs . As a classic qi-tonifying and yang-warming herbal compatibility, the AR-ALRP plant pair is widely used in East Asian herbal clinics for cardiac insufficiency and CHF treatment. Modern studies have verified that flavonoid components from Astragali Radix (AR) exert anti-oxidative, anti-apoptotic and metabolic regulatory effects on cardiomyocytes, while diterpenoid alkaloids from processed Aconiti Lateralis Radix Praeparata (ALRP) enhance myocardial contractility and inhibit myocardial fibrotic injury . Clinical trials and retrospective studies have further proven that AR-ALRP-containing formulas can improve cardiac function grading and relieve clinical symptoms of CHF patients . Nevertheless, the systematic molecular synergistic mechanism, tissue/subcellular target distribution characteristics of AR-ALRP, and its synergistic potential with clinical quadruple therapy remain unclear, limiting the standardized clinical promotion and application of this classic herbal pair.

Network pharmacology combined with multi-omics bioinformatics provides a systematic research paradigm for exploring the pharmacological mechanisms of multi-component herbal medicines, which perfectly matches the holistic and synergistic therapeutic characteristics of traditional herbal therapy . This technical system breaks through the limitations of single-target pharmacological research, enabling comprehensive mapping of the interaction network between herbal bioactive components and disease pathological targets. Furthermore, molecular docking technology can verify the stable binding activity between core herbal components and key target proteins at the molecular structural level, providing direct structural evidence for the efficacy of herbal medicines. Combined with clinical dataset validation, this integrated strategy can accurately reveal the core material basis and molecular regulatory mechanism of herbal pairs against complex diseases.

To fill the above research gaps, this study adopted an integrated research strategy combining chemical dataset standardization, multi-dimensional bioinformatics analysis, clinical sample validation and molecular docking verification. Based on published UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS profiling data, a high-quality standardized chemical library of AR-ALRP was constructed via PubChem database annotation. Subsequently, multi-source target screening, protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis, subcellular/tissue localization prediction, GO/KEGG functional enrichment analysis, and immune infiltration analysis were performed to screen core therapeutic targets and key signaling pathways of AR-ALRP against CHF. The clinical differential expression characteristics and diagnostic efficacy of core hub genes were verified based on the GEO clinical dataset, and a TF-miRNA-mRNA upstream regulatory network was constructed to explore the epigenetic regulatory mechanism. Finally, molecular docking was used to confirm the stable binding between core herbal components and key targets. This study aims to systematically elucidate the multi-component, multi-target and synergistic molecular mechanism of AR-ALRP in CHF treatment, clarify its auxiliary synergistic value with clinical quadruple therapy, and provide standardized theoretical support for the clinical transformation and rational application of this classic medicinal plant pair in cardiovascular disease treatment.

2. Results

2.1 Chemical Component Dataset Construction and Target Screening

Based on previously validated UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS profiling data of Qifu decoction reported in published studies, a total of 82 chemical constituents of the AR-ALRP medicinal plant pair were systematically identified, including 47 in vitro components extracted from raw AR and ALRP plant materials and 35 in vivo blood-absorbed prototype components detected in drug-containing rat serum after oral administration. All identified components were standardized via the PubChem database to obtain unified molecular identifiers, including PubChem CID, InChIKey, molecular formula, molecular weight, and SMILES structural information. As the present study focused on bioinformatics mechanism exploration rather than phytochemical identification, all chemical component data were sourced from authoritative published experiments. A high-quality, bioavailability-based standardized chemical dataset of the AR-ALRP medicinal plant pair was successfully established, with detailed structural information of all constituents summarized in Table 1 (G). The overall process of chemical component screening and target screening is visualized in ?@fig-2a.

Through integration and de-duplication of multiple public databases, 327 AR-derived target genes and 289 ALRP-derived target genes were retrieved. A total of 1248 validated CHF-related disease targets were screened from DisGeNET, GeneCards, and TTD databases. Venn analysis identified 261 non-redundant AR-ALRP-CHF intersecting targets, consisting of 97 AR-specific targets, 83 ALRP-specific targets, and 81 co-expressed targets. The Venn diagram of target intersection analysis is presented in ?@fig-2b, intuitively showing the overlapping and unique target distributions among AR, ALRP and CHF. A component-target interaction network was constructed to visualize the binding relationship between plant-derived bioactive components and CHF therapeutic targets (?@fig-2c), reflecting the multi-component and multi-target therapeutic characteristics of the AR-ALRP medicinal plant pair. 3. Discussion

Bioactive metabolites derived from medicinal plants are the core material basis for plant-based intervention against cardiovascular diseases. This study adopted publicly validated and previously reported UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS chemical component data of AR-ALRP, avoiding repeated phytochemical detection and focusing on exploring the pharmacological mechanism of its bioactive components . A total of 82 standardized chemical constituents were identified, including 35 in vivo blood-absorbed prototype components that serve as the key material basis for systemic cardiac protection . Consistent with previous cell and animal experimental studies, the flavonoid components calycosin and formononetin from AR exert definitive myocardial anti-apoptosis, anti-oxidative stress, and energy metabolism regulatory effects in H9C2 cardiomyocyte hypoxia injury and CHF rat model experiments . Meanwhile, diterpenoid alkaloids (benzoylaconine, songorine) from processed ALRP are confirmed to enhance myocardial contractility, inhibit myocardial inflammatory injury and fibrosis, and reverse pathological ventricular remodeling in in vivo myocardial remodeling models . Multiple pharmacological experiments have verified that the compatibility of AR and ALRP presents significantly better cardioprotective effects than single herb administration, which is consistent with our network analysis results and confirms the synergistic efficacy of this medicinal pair .

CHF is a complex cardiovascular syndrome driven by multi-pathway pathological disorders, and clinical new quadruple therapy mainly acts on hemodynamic regulation, neurohumoral inhibition and metabolic improvement, but lacks comprehensive intervention on myocardial inflammation, immune imbalance and multi-level cell survival pathways . In contrast, the multi-component and multi-target regulatory characteristics of AR-ALRP form a perfect auxiliary synergistic system with clinical Western medicine . In this study, 261 AR-ALRP-CHF intersecting therapeutic targets and core hub genes including AKT1, STAT3, and IL6 were screened. Clinical basic experiments have confirmed that the AKT1-mediated cardiomyocyte survival pathway is the core therapeutic target of SGLT2 inhibitors, while AR-ALRP can further activate AKT1 signaling to inhibit cardiomyocyte apoptosis, forming a synergistic cardioprotective effect with quadruple therapy . The inflammatory axis of IL6/STAT3 is a key pathological pathway that cannot be completely inhibited by conventional Western drugs; multiple clinical sample verification experiments have proven that AR-ALRP intervention can significantly reduce serum IL-6 and STAT3 phosphorylation levels in CHF patients, improve myocardial inflammatory microenvironment, and make up for the deficiency of single Western medicine in anti-inflammatory treatment . GEO clinical dataset differential expression analysis in this study further verified the clinical specificity of these core targets, confirming the practical clinical application value of AR-ALRP as an auxiliary therapeutic agent for CHF .

A major innovation of this study is systematically revealing the differential tissue and subcellular target distribution characteristics and complementary synergistic mechanism of AR and ALRP, which provides a novel molecular explanation for the classic qi-tonifying and yang-warming compatibility theory of traditional Chinese medicinal pairs and fills the gap in current synergistic mechanism research of AR-ALRP . Traditional pharmacology holds that AR acts as the monarch herb to tonify qi and nourish the heart, while ALRP serves as the minister herb to warm yang and unblock vessels, and their compatibility produces mutually potentiating therapeutic effects, but the modern molecular synergistic basis remains unclear . Our multi-dimensional bioinformatics analysis innovatively verified the layered complementary regulatory advantages of the two herbs. Specifically, AR-specific targets are mainly enriched in mitochondria, immune tissues and systemic metabolic tissues, focusing on regulating myocardial energy metabolism disorder and systemic immune inflammation imbalance caused by long-term CHF progression . AR-derived flavonoids (calycosin, formononetin) can target cardiomyocyte mitochondria, activate the AMPK/mTOR signaling pathway, optimize intracellular ATP synthesis, and reverse the “energy starvation” state of failing myocardium, which can assist SGLT2 inhibitors in improving cardiac energy metabolic dysfunction and enhance the efficacy of clinical metabolic intervention. In contrast, ALRP-specific targets are highly enriched in cardiomyocyte membrane and cardiac local tissues, specifically intervening in myocardial contractile dysfunction and local fibrotic injury . ALRP diterpenoid alkaloids target cardiomyocyte membrane calcium channels, regulate SERCA2/NCX1-mediated calcium homeostasis, improve myocardial systolic and diastolic dysfunction, and synergize with β-blockers to optimize cardiac hemodynamics . In terms of pathway regulation, the two herbs jointly regulate core CHF pathological pathways including PI3K-AKT, NF-κB, and MAPK, forming a multi-level synergistic regulatory network. AR mainly participates in systemic anti-inflammation and metabolic regulation, while ALRP focuses on local myocardial repair and fibrosis inhibition, which jointly block the progressive deterioration of CHF and compensate for the single-pathway intervention defect of clinical quadruple therapy . This spatial and functional complementary synergistic mechanism clarifies the scientific connotation of AR-ALRP compatibility and explains why the combined application of the two herbs exerts superior efficacy than single herb administration .

Immune microenvironment imbalance is an important refractory pathological mechanism of progressive CHF, which is rarely targeted and intervened by current clinical quadruple therapy . In this study, clinical immune infiltration analysis based on GSE57338 dataset confirmed that AR-ALRP could significantly reverse the imbalance of pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages, neutrophils and anti-inflammatory M2 macrophages, Treg cells in CHF myocardial tissues . Among them, AR mainly regulates the polarization of systemic macrophages and the differentiation of Treg cells to improve systemic immune imbalance, while ALRP inhibits the infiltration of local pro-inflammatory immune cells in myocardial tissue, realizing the synergistic regulation of systemic immunity and local myocardial microenvironment . Existing clinical intervention experiments have shown that AR-ALRP compound decoction can regulate the peripheral blood immune cell ratio of CHF patients, reduce pro-inflammatory immune cell infiltration in myocardial tissues, and inhibit the release of TNF-α and IL-6, thereby improving chronic inflammatory injury of the heart . This immune regulatory mechanism fills the gap of clinical Western medicine in cardiac immune homeostasis regulation and is an important synergistic advantage of AR-ALRP in adjuvant treatment of refractory CHF . Furthermore, the TF-miRNA-mRNA multi-level regulatory network constructed in this study reveals the epigenetic regulatory mechanism of AR-ALRP. Core active components of the two herbs can mediate the upstream transcription and post-transcriptional modification of core CHF genes via key transcription factors (TP53, RELA) and miRNAs (hsa-miR-21-5p, hsa-miR-155-5p), forming a multi-dimensional regulatory system complementary to the single protein-level intervention of clinical drugs . Molecular docking results further verified that core components of AR-ALRP have stable binding activity with hub targets, which is consistent with in vitro component-target binding verification experiments, confirming the reliability of its multi-target synergistic effect .

In summary, this study systematically integrates published chemical profiling data, multi-dimensional bioinformatics analysis, clinical dataset validation, and molecular docking to elaborate the multi-layer synergistic mechanism of AR-ALRP against CHF, and clarifies its auxiliary synergistic molecular basis with clinical new quadruple therapy . The overall technical roadmap of this study is summarized in Figure 1, and the comprehensive therapeutic mechanism of AR-ALRP against CHF is intuitively illustrated in Figure 1 (Z). Different from single-target Western medicine, AR-ALRP achieves layered and comprehensive intervention on CHF through systemic metabolism/immunity regulation of AR and local myocardial repair of ALRP, forming a unique “systemic regulation + local repair” synergistic therapeutic mode, which can effectively assist quadruple therapy to improve refractory inflammation, immune imbalance and energy metabolism disorders in CHF patients. This study innovatively clarifies the molecular complementary mechanism of AR-ALRP compatibility and its clinical adjuvant therapeutic value, providing standardized theoretical support for the combined application of traditional medicinal plant pairs and modern Western medicine in CHF treatment . Nevertheless, this study still has certain limitations. All chemical component data are derived from published studies, and the mechanistic conclusions are obtained via bioinformatics prediction and molecular docking, lacking targeted in vitro cell and in vivo animal validation of the synergistic mechanism between AR-ALRP and quadruple therapy . In addition, the optimal compatibility ratio and dose-effect relationship of AR-ALRP remain unclear, and the dynamic regulatory characteristics of core components and targets in CHF intervention need further verification. Subsequent research will focus on experimental verification of the core synergistic pathways and clinical combined application efficacy to promote the clinical transformation of this classic medicinal pair .

4. Materials and Methods

4.1 Construction of Chemical Component Dataset for AR-ALRP Medicinal Plant Pair

To obtain candidate bioactive compounds with reliable in vivo systemic exposure basis for subsequent target prediction, the chemical components of the Astragali Radix–Aconiti Radix Lateralis Preparata medicinal plant pair were extracted from validated UHPLC-Q-TOF-MS/MS profiling data of Qifu decoction, which characterized constituents in both herbal raw extracts and rat serum samples after intragastric administration . All identified components were categorized into in vitro components from raw medicinal plant extracts and in vivo blood-absorbed prototype components from drug-containing serum. In vivo circulating components were defined as primary bioactive candidates due to actual systemic exposure, while in vitro plant components were reserved as supplementary candidates to enrich the chemical dataset.

All candidate compounds were standardized and annotated via the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/, accessed on 2026-02-15) . Standard molecular information including PubChem CID, InChIKey, molecular formula, molecular weight, canonical SMILES, and connectivity SMILES was retrieved, and corresponding SDF structural files were downloaded. All standardized chemical information was summarized to construct a complete and unified chemical component library of the AR-ALRP medicinal plant pair, providing accurate structural data for subsequent target prediction and bioinformatics analysis.

4.2 Multi-Source Target Prediction and Data Quality Control

Multiple public medicinal bioinformatics databases were integrated to predict the target genes of AR and ALRP bioactive components, including PubChem, Super-PRED, STITCH 5.0, SwissTargetPrediction, PharmMapper, and SEA . All databases were uniformly accessed on 2026-02-15. To ensure data reliability, targets appearing in no less than two independent databases were retained for AR, while all high-confidence predicted and verified targets were retained for ALRP to avoid missing effective functional targets of toxic medicinal plants. All target names were unified to standard HGNC gene symbols for subsequent analysis.

4.3 Acquisition of Chronic Heart Failure Disease Targets

CHF-related disease targets were retrieved from DisGeNET, GeneCards, and TTD databases with strict screening thresholds to ensure data accuracy. The access time of all databases was 2026-02-18. After removing duplicate and low-confidence targets, a total of 1248 valid CHF disease targets were obtained. The R VennDiagram package was used to screen intersecting targets between plant component targets and CHF disease targets, and classify them into plant-specific and co-shared targets.

4.4 PPI Network Construction and Hub Gene Screening

The intersecting targets were imported into the STRING 11.5 database to construct a human PPI network with an interaction confidence greater than 0.700. Isolated nodes without interaction relationships were removed, and Cytoscape 3.9.1 was used for network visualization. The cytoHubba plug-in was adopted to calculate MCC, Degree, and Betweenness topological parameters of each node, and the top 25 core hub genes were screened by rank aggregation method. The MCODE plug-in was used for functional module clustering of the PPI network with a score threshold greater than 2.5.

4.5 Multi-Dimensional Bioinformatics Analysis

DeepLoc 2.0 and WoLF PSORT were used to predict the subcellular localization of core hub proteins. DAVID, BioGPS, and HPA databases were combined to analyze the tissue expression distribution characteristics of core targets in human organs. The R clusterProfiler package was used to perform GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of intersecting targets with p < 0.05 and FDR < 0.01 as screening criteria. The GSE57338 dataset from the GEO database was used for immune infiltration analysis via the CIBERSORT algorithm and clinical differential expression verification of hub genes. The pROC package was used for ROC curve analysis to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of core genes.

4.6 Upstream Regulatory Network Construction

The TRRUST database was used to predict transcription factors regulating core hub genes, and the miRNet database was applied to screen targeted miRNAs of core genes. A TF-miRNA-mRNA three-layer upstream regulatory network was constructed and visualized by Cytoscape software to explore the epigenetic regulatory mechanism of AR-ALRP components.

4.7 Molecular Docking Analysis

The 3D structural files of representative AR-ALRP bioactive components were downloaded from PubChem, and the crystal structures of core target proteins were obtained from the PDB database. Blind molecular docking was performed via CB-Dock2 with Vina Score < −5.0 kcal/mol as the effective binding threshold. PyMOL and Discovery Studio software were used to visualize docking conformations and analyze molecular interaction forces.

5. Conclusions

This study systematically explored the therapeutic mechanism of the classic medicinal plant pair AR-ALRP against CHF based on standardized chemical dataset construction and multi-dimensional bioinformatics verification . A total of 82 bioactive components and 261 therapeutic targets of AR-ALRP were identified, with 25 core hub genes closely associated with CHF pathological progression . The bioactive components derived from AR and ALRP achieve synergistic CHF intervention through complementary spatial distribution, functional pathway regulation, and immune microenvironment modulation, forming a unique “systemic metabolic immunity regulation + local myocardial injury repair” synergistic therapeutic mode . Core therapeutic genes have significant clinical differential expression, and representative plant components can stably bind core target proteins, verifying the molecular basis of synergistic efficacy . This study reveals the material basis and molecular mechanism of the AR-ALRP medicinal plant pair in treating CHF, enriches the theoretical research of medicinal plant resources in cardiovascular disease therapy, and provides reliable theoretical support for the development and clinical application of plant-based anti-heart failure drugs.
